03

The whole course in three moves

The concept

Every phrase in this course does one of three things. It builds your presence (how people see you), it helps your communication land (so people understand and act), or it adds to your career velocity (so your work and ambitions are noticed). Browse the best phrase from each lesson, then sort them in the quick check.

Click either cell to see another example.

Presence: follow the other person's lead on names and formality; soften requests and disagreements; decode understatement.
Communication: put the point first; chase lightly; get the floor with a short apology; disagree with acknowledge → concern → suggestion; say no with a reason and an alternative; apologise by owning it first.
Career velocity: results and numbers, "we" and shared credit, no surprises for your manager, and ask for the conversation before you ask for the thing.
The one rule behind all of it
be clear about the message and generous about the person. Blunt speakers forget the second half; over-polite speakers forget the first. Every "just right" example in this course does both.

If you're ever unsure, three questions help: Would they know exactly what I want? Would they feel respected? Would I say this to someone senior?

04

Monday to Friday

Reading

Monday. Zofia's first day at a London fintech began with a round of introductions. When the head of product said "Hi, I'm Rob," she answered “Nice to meet you, Rob”, no title, no surname, and asked him how long he'd been with the company. By lunchtime she had a rapport with half the team, mostly because she'd asked questions and listened to the answers.

Tuesday and Wednesday. She needed data from a team she'd never worked with, so she wrote “I was wondering if you could send me the Q3 figures when you get a chance?” They arrived within the hour. On Wednesday evening she joined Rob at a client dinner. When the client raised politics over dessert, Zofia smiled, said it had been quite a year, and asked about his holiday plans instead. Her discretion did not go unnoticed.

Thursday. In the strategy meeting, the director proposed doubling the marketing budget. Zofia thought the numbers didn't support it. She waited for a natural pause, then said “Sorry to jump in — I take the point, but I'm a bit worried about the return on the last campaign.” The room paused, then agreed to look at the data again. That afternoon she discovered she had sent the client an old version of a proposal. Her email began “Apologies — I attached the wrong version, and that's my mistake.” It was three lines long. The client replied with a smiley face.

Friday. Rob stopped by her desk. "Great first week," he said. "The client loved you." Zofia said “Thanks — Jess's figures made the dinner conversation much easier”, and then, because the moment felt right, added “I'd love the chance to lead the next client project. What would you need to see from me?” Rob looked taken aback for a second, then smiled and gave her two things to work on. She sent him a short follow-up email that afternoon summarising them. None of it was rocket science. It was simply the unwritten rules, followed consistently, for five days in a row.
